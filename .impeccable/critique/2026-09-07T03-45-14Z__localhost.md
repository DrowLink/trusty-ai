## Design Specificity Verdict

**LLM Assessment**: The product has strong bones and a clear identity ambition — "VirusTotal meets Moody's for AI agents" — but the execution doesn't yet reflect that institutional authority. The hero is now spacious and well-structured. But across tabs, the product reads as a competent Next.js SaaS template with a branded color swap, not a conviction-led fintech instrument. The violations are concentrated: purple accent that belongs to no core identity, gradient text on pricing headlines, inconsistent light-mode treatment, and the absence of any information-architecture hierarchy inside the leaderboard. The hero animation pipeline is the highest-specificity element on the page — it's genuinely product-authored. Everything else could belong to any AI-adjacent tool.

**Deterministic Scan**: 19 findings across 7 files.
- **11 `ai-color-palette`** warnings: `text-purple-200/400/600`, `from-purple-500/600` gradients in `AgentCreditCard`, `AgentDetailModal`, `BusinessAndPricingView`, `EconomicCaseView`, `UnderwritingExplainer`. These are the most recognizable tells of AI-generated UI. The product's stated palette is blue (`#0066FF`) — purple is an uninvited guest.
- **7 `gray-on-color`** warnings: `text-slate-950 on bg-blue-600` in `HeroMetrics` (×2) and `Navbar` (×1); `text-slate-950 on bg-blue-50` in `EconomicCaseView`; `text-slate-950 on bg-sky-500` and `text-slate-950 on bg-purple-500` in `UnderwritingExplainer`; `text-zinc-400 on bg-rose-50` in `SpecsModal`. These are real contrast failures.
- **1 `gradient-text`** warning: `bg-clip-text + bg-gradient` in `BusinessAndPricingView` line 133 — the "Monetize decisions" heading. The PRODUCT.md brief explicitly bans "decorative gradients."

No false positives identified. The purple findings are accurate — the identity system has not been enforced consistently across all components.

## Overall Impression

The homepage has one dominant strength and one dominant failure. The strength: the hero section's spatial design and decision pipeline animation give the product a genuine identity moment — the clock, the latency, the verdict — this is authored. The failure: everything below the fold regresses to generic. Purple bleeds into the color system with no rationale. The pricing tab contradicts the brand brief with gradient text. The leaderboard is dense without hierarchy. The light-mode pass is incomplete — several tabs still render as dark-first, then fail in light mode. The biggest single opportunity is palette discipline: cut purple to zero, and this UI immediately reads as a tier more authoritative.

## What's Working

**1. Hero Decision Pipeline** — The animated scenario card is the most product-specific element on the page. The latency badge, circuit breaker grid, verdict banner with amount and rail, and scenario chips together compose a coherent narrative. This is what makes TRUSTY feel like infrastructure, not a dashboard.

**2. Navbar brand lockup** — "TRUSTY.bot™ / TRUST POWERS AGENTS" with the isotype is clean, confident, and communicates institutional authority at a glance. The floating pill style with frosted glass works well against both light and dark backgrounds.

## Priority Issues

**[P1] Purple palette contamination across 5 components**
- **What**: `text-purple-200/400/600`, `from-purple-500/600` gradients in `AgentCreditCard`, `AgentDetailModal`, `BusinessAndPricingView`, `EconomicCaseView`, and `UnderwritingExplainer`. 11 detector hits.
- **Why it matters**: PRODUCT.md commits to blue as the brand color and explicitly bans "decorative fluff." Purple is the single most recognizable tell of AI-generated UI. It reads as the product wasn't authored — it was generated and left uncleaned.
- **Fix**: Replace all `purple-*` colors with the brand blue (`#0066FF` / `sky-400` in dark). Specifically: heading highlights → `text-[#0066FF]` in light / `text-sky-400` in dark. Gradients → `from-[#0066FF] to-blue-400`. The `AgentDetailModal` score bar gradient should shift to a blue-to-sky sweep.
- **Suggested command**: `$impeccable colorize`

**[P1] Gradient text on pricing headline violates own brand brief**
- **What**: `BusinessAndPricingView.tsx:133` — "Monetize decisions." uses `bg-clip-text bg-gradient`. PRODUCT.md says zero decorative gradients; DESIGN.md bans this pattern explicitly.
- **Why it matters**: The only page that explicitly pitches the business model uses a visual pattern the brand has ruled out. It undercuts the institutional authority of the pricing message.
- **Fix**: Replace with a two-color split using solid text: "Monetize" in `text-slate-950` / "decisions." in `text-[#0066FF]`. No gradient.
- **Suggested command**: `$impeccable colorize`

**[P1] 7 gray-on-color contrast failures**
- **What**: `text-slate-950 on bg-blue-600` in CTAs (Navbar, HeroMetrics buttons); `text-slate-950 on bg-sky-500` and `text-slate-950 on bg-purple-500` in UnderwritingExplainer; `text-zinc-400 on bg-rose-50` in SpecsModal.
- **Why it matters**: `text-slate-950` (#0f172a) on `bg-blue-600` (#2563EB) fails WCAG AA at ~2.2:1. The primary CTA button text is failing contrast — this is a showstopper for accessibility.
- **Fix**: All text on solid colored backgrounds → `text-white`. Specifically: primary blue buttons → `text-white` (already correct in most places but wrong in Navbar mobile sheet and two HeroMetrics buttons); `bg-sky-500` → `text-white`; `bg-purple-500` → `text-white`; `bg-rose-50` → `text-rose-700` not `text-zinc-400`.
- **Suggested command**: `$impeccable audit`

**[P2] Incomplete light-mode treatment across tabs**
- **What**: DecisionPlayground header (fixed today), but UnderwritingExplainer, EconomicCaseView, and CompetitiveMoatView still use hardcoded dark-first classes (`text-white`, `text-slate-300`, `bg-slate-900`) without dark: prefixes.
- **Why it matters**: Every time the user switches to light mode, they encounter content that appears broken. The mode toggle is a prominent navbar control — inconsistency here undermines trust in the product's own quality signal.
- **Fix**: Systematic sweep: anywhere `text-white` appears outside a `dark:` prefix or outside an explicitly dark-background context, add `dark:text-white text-slate-900` pair.
- **Suggested command**: `$impeccable harden`

**[P2] API error handling uses browser `alert()`**
- **What**: `DecisionPlayground.tsx:75` — `alert(data.error || 'Failed to evaluate decision')`. Same pattern in several catch blocks.
- **Why it matters**: Browser `alert()` is a trust signal failure on a product selling trust. It looks like a prototype, not an institutional tool. It also blocks the UI entirely.
- **Fix**: Replace with an inline error state inside the result panel — a red-bordered card with the error message and a retry button. No blocking alerts.
- **Suggested command**: `$impeccable harden`

## Minor Observations

- The "Quick samples" search seeds ("ProcurementBot-847", "crewAI Repo", "anthropic-quickstarts") are excellent — they demonstrate the product immediately. Consider surfacing these even more prominently, perhaps as a row of discovery chips above the leaderboard.
- The footer (`src/app/page.tsx`) uses `TrustyIsotype` with a `text-[#0066FF]` className on an `<img>` tag — color className is meaningless on an img. The SVG in PartnerLogos should be inline SVG if color control via CSS is needed.
- The quota counter "10/10" in the Navbar reads ambiguously — does it mean "10 queries used out of 10" or "10 remaining out of 10"? Consider "10 left" or a draining progress ring.
- The `CookieConsent` component doesn't appear to have a decline or "preferences" path — EU/GDPR compliance gap.
- Page height is 16,273px (metadata). That is very long for a single-page app — no anchor links or section IDs in the URL, making it impossible to share a deep link to, say, the Pricing section.
